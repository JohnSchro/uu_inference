The point of this lab was to find a price point between zero dollars and seventy dollars that maximized our expected revenue per customer. For this lab the price does not affect whether a customer views the product however it does affect if they buy or simply pass over it meaning they either take the offered price or give zero revenue. The purchasing decision for each customer is drawn from a distribution so it is stochastic and gives us a noisy revenue curve. There are also multiple peaks meaning the curve is nonlinear with multiple local optima. Just because we observe a high revenue at a certain spot does not mean that it is the actual global optimum. For this lab I first manually played around with it until I understood how it worked and then I made an algorithmic process in the final cell putting together everything that I experimented with.

My final algorithmic search uses a seed and I also cache results. I decided that I was going to pretend this was a startup and have roughly 6000 customers to experiment with. My algorithm first starts with a grid spaced $5 apart and I use roughly 200 customers at each price. I then did two refinement rounds where I selected the two best local peaks and tested the prices on either side of them first at half of the initial spacing so $2.50 and then after that $1.25 from those new results. I went with this because it got the broad coverage that I wanted to search for a global optimum but I also included more local refinement to get closer to that optimum itself. The minimum spacing because of this ended up being $1.25 and I got my final value by taking the tested prices and finding the one with the highest observed mean revenue even if at the end of the test the difference might not have been statistically significant or there was overlap with other plausible values.

I decided to use two sided Z tests with 95% confidence intervals in my manual experimentation and then in my final algorithmic code block I went with 99%. Each of my intervals compared the current leader which was the price with the highest observed mean revenue per customer with other tested prices. I took the difference in means and then did plus or minus 2.576 times the standard error of that difference giving us a 99% confidence interval. I then knew that an interval containing zero meant that the comparison between those two prices was unresolved. I also knew that a 90% or 95% confidence interval would be a little narrower potentially ruling out competitors sooner while 99% would produce a wider interval and allow me to keep more uncertain competitors so I don't narrow the global space down too much. However this likely meant I needed more data. After the refinement steps the additional batches go to the leaders of that step or the unresolved competitors. So I cached the previous results and then given the additional results combined them to create a new estimated mean and a new estimated variance. This allowed me to direct more sampling towards estimates that I wasn't sure about instead of simply giving every price the exact same sample amount. I also reused the cached results whenever I tested a price again.

Again I cached these observations by price and if the new batches used the same price I combined the previous results with the new results to form one more precise estimate. So earlier data didn't need to be redone it was still useful and I kept it which was also included in the automatic search not just the manual search.

Another challenge that I faced was deciding how and when to stop despite uncertainty and overlapping confidence intervals between different price points. But because I'm searching for a global optimum I can never actually truly guarantee that I found the best price point. I have to make some assumptions about what happens between the prices I tested and whether local movements will help me approach the optimum.
